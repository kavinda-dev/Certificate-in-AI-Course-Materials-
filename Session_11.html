Concept 1

Every workflow has three parts

1

Trigger

"What starts it. A form is submitted."

2

Steps

"What happens next. The answer lands in a sheet and a formula calculates."

3

Output

"What you get. A total, a count, a report."

"Like a restaurant order ticket: the order comes in, the kitchen follows the steps, and the plate comes out."

workflow trigger automation (steps that run without you repeating them)
Concept 2

The intake counter and the ledger

Google Form

"The intake counter. Everyone gives information in the same structure."

Term: structured data

Google Sheet

"The ledger. Every answer becomes one row, and formulas do the adding up."

Term: formula (an instruction that calculates from cells)
Form response new row in the Sheet formulas update
Note: Google adds a Timestamp column at the start of every response sheet.
Concept 3

You describe it, Claude writes the formula

Analogy

"Like asking a colleague who is good at spreadsheets: 'I need the total for each order.'"

Technical Reality

Function: a built-in calculation such as SUM or COUNTIF.
Range: the group of cells it works on.
Cell reference: the address of a cell, such as F2.

How to ask (4 ingredients):

  • Say which column holds what
  • Say what you want to calculate
  • Give two sample rows
  • Say where the result goes
Claude can miscount columns. Always test a formula on numbers you can calculate by hand.
Concept 4

Describe a tool, Claude builds it

Analogy

"Like asking a developer colleague to build a small calculator for your team."

Technical Reality

Artifact: a small working page or tool that Claude builds next to the chat.
Prototype: a first working version you can test and improve.
Describe it Test it Ask for one change Test again
Note: Be specific. List what goes in, what comes out and what the screen should show.
Concept 5

Automation needs checking

Check the numbers

"Test with a case you can calculate by hand. If the tool says 1,700 and your answer is 1,700, you can trust it more."

Protect the data

"Do not collect customer phone numbers, addresses or payment details in a practice form. Use fictional data in class."

Automation repeats mistakes quickly. One wrong formula is wrong in every row.
validation (checking that data and results are correct) human-in-the-loop

Hands-on Lab

Your scenario

You work in Sales at Ceylon Fresh Foods (a fictional company). Orders arrive by message and get lost. You will build an order tracker that records each order and calculates the totals, plus a small tool for quoting order totals with a discount.

Rules for the lab

Use at most 8 prompts
  • Plan for 6 prompts, with 2 spare prompts only if time allows.
  • Use the Copy button. Do not retype.
  • Read each reply before moving on.
  • Use only the fictional data given in this guide.
Prompt 1 of 6

Design the form

Open a new chat in Claude and send this.

Design a Google Form with 7 questions for logging customer orders. The questions should be: Order ID, Date, Customer, Product, Quantity, Unit price in LKR, Status (Delivered, Late or Pending). For each question, say the best question type.

What to notice:

Claude suggests a question type for each, such as date, multiple choice and short answer.

Prompt 2 of 6

The formulas

Back in your Claude chat, send this.

My Google Sheet is linked to a Google Form. Google adds a Timestamp column, so my columns are: A Timestamp, B Order ID, C Date, D Customer, E Product, F Quantity, G Unit price LKR, H Status. Row 1 is the header row. Give me formulas for: the total value of each order in column I, the total sales, the number of Late orders, and the total sales for Mango Jam. Here are two sample rows, starting from column B:
1001, 2026-10-01, Dilani, Mango Jam, 2, 850, Delivered
1002, 2026-10-02, Hotel Lagoon, Mango Jam, 50, 780, Late

What to notice:

Does each formula point at the right columns? Compare with your column letters.

Prompt 3 of 6

Where each formula goes

Give me step-by-step instructions to add these formulas to my Sheet: say exactly which cell each formula goes into, and how to make the totals work for new rows added later.

Then: Follow Claude's steps and paste each formula into the cell it names.

What to notice:

Formulas that cover a whole column keep working when new orders arrive.

Review

Check the formulas against numbers you can calculate

No prompt here. Compare your sheet with these expected results.

Results table

  • Total value of order 1001: 1,700 (2 x 850)
  • Total value of order 1002: 39,000 (50 x 780)
  • Total sales: 40,700
  • Number of Late orders: 1
  • Total sales for Mango Jam: 40,700

Checklist

  • Every result matches the table
  • The total sales formula would include new rows
  • The formulas are in the right cells
Note: If a number is wrong, tell Claude which cell and what you expected, then try again. This is the same loop as Session 8.
