Welcome and warm-up

CAIP01 Goal

Understand AI, try it, use it safely. No technical background needed.

Ground Rule

No confidential or personal data in any tool today.

Ask in Pairs

Where did you use AI this week? Then a show of hands: daily, sometimes, never.

"Everyone is at a different level, and that is fine."
Concept

What is AI? (in plain words)

Analogy

"Think of an intern who has read millions of documents but has never worked in your office."

AI

Software that learns patterns from many examples, then predicts or creates.

It does not think or understand like a person.

You already use it: spam filters, maps, bank fraud alerts, streaming suggestions.

Definitions

AI, machine learning, generative AI

AI

The big idea.

Machine learning

AI that learns from examples, such as fraud detection.

Generative AI

Creates new text, images or audio, like ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot and Claude.

Quick Poll: AI or not AI?

  • Calculator
  • Excel SUM
  • Spam filter
  • Chatbot
"Fixed rules are not AI. Learned patterns are."
How It Works

How AI works: learn, then predict

Learn

It is trained on huge amounts of text, so it learns patterns, not a filing cabinet of facts.

Predict

It writes one word at a time, like phone autocomplete on a far bigger scale.

The Result

  • Fluent text that sounds sure, whether or not it is true.
  • Its knowledge stops at a training date unless the tool searches the web.

Capabilities

Strengths and limits

Analogy

"A brilliant, overconfident intern who rarely says 'I don't know'."

Strengths

First drafts, summaries, brainstorming, rewording, explaining hard ideas simply.

Hallucinations

Confident answers that are wrong or invented.

Also limits:

Out-of-date information, bias from its training data, and no knowledge of your organisation.

Risk Framework

Traffic-light guide and the spot-check habit

Green

Brainstorming, rewording, drafts.

Amber

Facts, numbers, legal or medical content, so verify everything.

Red

Decisions about people, confidential data, final sign-off, which stay with humans.

Before you trust it, ask: Can I check it? What if it is wrong? Who is accountable? (You are.)
In 2023, New York lawyers were fined $5,000 for submitting case citations that ChatGPT invented.
Activity

Activity 1, Test the AI

Part A 5 min, Part B 6 min, Debrief 4 min 15 min

Work in pairs and pick one tool: Gemini, Copilot, Claude or ChatGPT.

Part A - Strength test

Summarise the memo below in 3 bullet points for a busy manager, then list every date in a small table. Memo: "Greenfield Supplies will move its monthly stock count to the first Monday of each month from November. Branch managers must submit count sheets by 5 p.m. that Monday. Training runs on 28 and 29 October at 10 a.m. in the main conference room."

Part B - Trap test

Give me three published research studies, with authors, year, journal and link, showing that four-day work weeks improve productivity in call centres.

Then search for each reference online and mark it real, partly right or invented.

Debrief

How many could you verify? Did it sound confident? What check will you add to your own work?

Tool Selection

Choosing the right AI

No single best tool. Choose by task, not by brand, and test with your own task.

Ask four questions:

  • What is the task?
  • Where does my work live?
  • How sensitive is the data?
  • What if it is wrong?
Tool Good starting use
Copilot Outlook, Word, Teams, if your organisation licenses it
Gemini Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive
Claude Long documents, careful writing
ChatGPT Flexible all-rounder for ideas and drafts
"For important work, try two tools and compare."
Skill Building

Learn AI fast and keep it a helper

Practise 15 minutes a day on a real task, and keep a prompt library

Improve results with follow-ups: "shorter", "more formal", "give me three options"

The loop: you set the goal, AI drafts, you check, you decide

Analogy

"AI changes tasks. Your judgement, checking and people skills are your advantage."

Ethics & Safety

Why responsible AI matters, and bias

AI speeds up good work and also speeds up mistakes. You, not the tool, are accountable.

AI learns from human data, so it can copy human unfairness.

Reuters reported in 2018 that Amazon dropped an experimental recruiting tool that downgraded CVs mentioning women's activities.

Never let AI alone decide about hiring, pay, grades or care.
Ethics & Safety

Privacy and confidentiality

Analogy

"Treat free public tools like a postcard, not a sealed envelope."

  • Do not paste personal, client, patient or financial data into unapproved tools.
  • Anonymise first, and follow your organisation's policy.
Test: "Would I pin this on the office noticeboard?"
Ethics & Safety

Copyright and honesty

Do not publish AI text untouched, since it may resemble existing work.

Rewrite in your own voice, and cite real sources you have checked yourself.

Say when you used AI if your policy or clients expect it.

Case Study

Mini case: the airline chatbot

The Facts

  • In 2022, Air Canada's website chatbot told a grieving customer he could claim a bereavement discount after travel. This was wrong.
  • In 2024 a Canadian tribunal held the airline responsible and ordered it to pay him C$812.02.

Discussion

  • What went wrong?
  • Who is accountable?
  • What would you have checked?
Framework

The 5 Golden Rules

1

Protect

No confidential or personal data in unapproved tools.

2

Verify

Check facts, figures, names and sources.

3

Own it

You are accountable, and humans decide on people.

4

Disclose

Be open about AI use where it matters.

5

Be fair

Look for bias and respect copyright.

Activity

Activity 2, Build your own prompt

12 min

Steps

  1. Pick a task from your own job. Use made-up details only.
  2. Write a 5-part prompt and run it (4 min).
  3. Improve it with a follow-up: Make it shorter and more formal. or List anything I should double-check. (3 min)
  4. Check the result against the Golden Rules, and underline three things to verify (3 min).
  5. Tell your partner one thing AI did well and one thing you had to fix (2 min).

Activity prompts are on your handout or in the shared chat.

Sample starter prompt (HR)

You are an experienced HR officer. Write a job description for a Customer Service Executive at a mid-sized retail company. Context: handles phone and email queries, reports to the Customer Service Manager, needs 1-2 years' experience. Format: short intro, six responsibilities, five requirements. Limits: under 250 words, plain language, no gender-specific words.
Summary

What we covered

AI finds patterns and generates text. It does not look things up or truly understand.
Great for drafts, summaries and ideas, but verify the rest.
Pick tools by task, data sensitivity and where you work.
Use AI to augment yourself, and keep the 5 Golden Rules.
