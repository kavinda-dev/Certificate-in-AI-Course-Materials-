Concept 1

You stay in charge

Analogy

"A pilot using autopilot. The autopilot flies, but the pilot watches, and decides when to take over."

Technical Reality

Human-in-the-loop

A person reviews and approves the AI's work before it is used.

Approval point

The moment where a person must say yes.

Three approval points

Before anything is sent to a customer or manager
Before numbers are used in a decision
Before anything is saved, shared or published
Concept 2

Protect the information you give AI

Safe to share

"Practice data, public information, anonymised text, your own drafts."

Do not share

"Customer details, passwords, financial records, HR files, private contracts, confidential strategy."

"Treat what you type into AI like a message you would be comfortable having read by others."

Rule: Follow your workplace's AI policy. If there is none, ask before using real documents.
personal data confidential information anonymise (remove names and identifying details)
Concept 3

Confident does not mean correct

Analogy

"A confident new intern who would rather guess than say 'I don't know'."

Technical Reality

Hallucination

Made-up facts stated with confidence.

Verification

Checking a claim against a real source.

Three habits (from this course)

Ask for sources, then open them (Session 9).
Tell the AI to write NOT STATED instead of guessing (Session 10).
Test numbers against a calculation you can do by hand (Session 11).
Concept 4

AI can repeat unfair patterns

Analogy

"A colleague who has only read certain books will describe the world through those books."

Technical Reality

Bias

An unfair pattern in an AI's output that comes from the data it learned from, or from how a question is worded.

Where it shows up

Descriptions of people or groups
Hiring, grading or performance wording
Examples and names that always come from one culture
Check question: "Is anyone described unfairly, or left out?"
Concept 5

Know the limits

Use AI to draft, summarise, organise and check

"You review the result."

Do not use AI alone for

"Decisions about people (hiring, discipline), legal, medical or financial advice, anything involving confidential data."

Accountability: If you send it, you own it. The AI is never the one responsible for the result.

Lab: Your Capstone

Your capstone task

Combine what you built into one kit that someone else could pick up and use, then check it for risk.

Steps

  • Gather your items (Slide 11)
  • Build the index (Prompt 1)
  • Write the SOP (Prompt 2)
  • Review against the checklist (Prompt 3)
  • Fix and present

Rules

Use at most 4 prompts
  • Plan for 3 prompts, with 1 spare prompt if you need it.
  • Use the Copy button.
  • Read each reply before moving on.
List Your Items

One line for each item

Write one short line for each item in your own notes. These lines go into Prompt 1.

1. Weekly update prompt: what it does
2. Customer reply assistant: what it does
3. Meeting-to-action prompt: what it does
4. Research method: what it does
5. Order tracker: what it does
6. Discount tool: what it does
Tip: Keep each line to one sentence.
Prompt 1 of 4

Build the index

Open a new chat in Claude. Replace the six lines below with your own lines from Slide 11, or leave them as they are to use the sample kit.

Here are my saved prompts, my assistant and my tools from this course. Organise them into a one-page index called "My AI Productivity Kit".
1. Weekly update prompt: Goal: write the weekly update email to my manager. Steps: group facts into Results, Problems and Next week; write the email; check every number. Format: under 150 words.
2. Customer reply prompt: Goal: write a short reply to a customer whose order is late. Steps: apologise, offer 10% off the next order, give a next step. Format: under 80 words.
3. Meeting-to-action prompt: Summarise the notes in 5 bullets, create a task table (Task, Owner, Deadline, Status; write NOT STATED if missing), then write a follow-up email under 150 words.
4. Customer Reply Assistant: a Claude Project that follows our style guide, replies in under 120 words, and never promises anything not in company policy.
5. Order tracker: a Google Form feeding a Google Sheet with formulas for order totals and late orders.
6. Discount tool: a small web tool that calculates the order total with a discount.

What to notice: A clear, short index. Can someone else tell what each item is for?

Prompt 2 of 4

The SOP

SOP: standard operating procedure, a short written set of steps so anyone can do the task the same way. 
Write a one-page SOP for this kit: for each item, say when to use it, the steps to use it, and when NOT to use AI. Keep it simple for a busy office worker.

What to notice:

Each item has a 'when NOT to use AI' line. Are they sensible?

Prompt 3 of 4

Risk review

Review my kit against this checklist and list anything missing or risky:
- No confidential, personal or customer data was pasted into the AI
- Facts, numbers and dates were checked against a real source
- A human read and approved the output before it was sent or used
- The output follows our company policy
- Bias check: is anyone described unfairly or left out?
- Tone and wording match how I would normally write
- I know what the AI could not do or did not know
- I saved the final prompt so I can reuse it

What to notice:

The AI finds gaps, but you decide which ones matter.

Fix and Finish

Fix what the review found

Checklist

  • Add or change at least one line in your SOP based on the review
  • Make sure each item says when a human must approve it
  • Remove anything confidential from your examples
Prompt 4 of 4 (only if needed) 
Shorten the SOP so it fits on one page and add a final line: "A human must check every output before it is used."
Note: Save your index and SOP in one document called My AI Productivity Kit.
