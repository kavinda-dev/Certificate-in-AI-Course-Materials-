Concept 1

What is a research agent?

Analogy

"A research intern. You give them a question, they search, read several sources, compare what they find and report back with references."

Technical Reality

Web search

The AI looks up live information instead of relying only on what it learned in training.

Grounding

Tying each answer to a source you can open.

Without web search Answers from memory. May be out of date.
With web search Looks up current pages and gives links.
Concept 2

A good research brief has four parts

"Like briefing a research intern before they start."

Decision

"What are you trying to decide?"

Criteria

"How will you judge the options? (price, features, fit)"

Output

"What should the result look like? (a table and one recommendation)"

Evidence

"A source link for every fact."

criteria structured brief

Concept 4

Where research agents fail

"Like an intern who cites a book they never opened."

Made-up facts

"States a price or feature the source never mentions." (hallucination)

Mismatched sources

"The link exists, but it does not say what is claimed."

Out-of-date information

"Prices and features change."

Weak sources

"Blogs and forums used instead of the official page."

A link is not proof. You open it and check.
Concept 5

Three steps to check a claim

1

Open the source link.

2

Find the sentence that supports the claim.

3

Mark it: Correct, Partly correct or Wrong.

Source Ladder (Best to Weakest)
Official website / pricing page Reputable news / review site Blog Forum
Also check the date and the currency.

Hands-on Lab

Your scenario

A small business in Sri Lanka with 5 staff needs an accounting or invoicing tool that costs under LKR 5,000 per month. You will research three options and recommend one.

Rules for the lab

Use at most 6 prompts
  • Plan for 6 prompts in total.
  • Turn on web search before Prompt 1.
  • Send one prompt at a time and read the reply before the next.
  • Use the Copy button. Do not retype.
Session 9 • Step 1

Turn on web search

Claude • Web search
1

Start a new chat in Claude.

2

In the message box, open the tools menu (often a "+" or settings icon).

3

Switch Web search on.

The menu may look slightly different on your screen. Look for the words Web search. If you cannot find it, tell the lecturer.

Prompt 1 of 6

Research

Research this decision: a small business in Sri Lanka needs an accounting or invoicing tool that costs under LKR 5,000 per month. Compare 3 options on price, features and suitability for a business with 5 staff. Give a comparison table, one recommendation, and list the source link for every fact.

What to notice:

Does every fact have a link? Is the table clear? Are the prices in LKR?

Prompt 2 of 6

Summarise for a manager

Rewrite the recommendation in 100 words for a manager. Keep the source links.

What to notice:

A short version for a busy reader, with the evidence still attached.

Session 9 • Step 2

Pick a claim to check

Claude • Your research reply
  1. In Claude's reply, choose one sentence with a fact in it, such as a price, a feature or a limit.
  2. Copy that sentence.
  3. In the next prompt box, replace the word CLAIM with your sentence.
Tip: Pick a different claim for each check: a price, a feature, and a limit or condition.

Worked example

If Claude wrote: 'Option A costs LKR 4,200 per month.' then you send:

Show me exactly where in the cited source it says this: Option A costs LKR 4,200 per month. If the source does not say it, tell me clearly.
Prompts 3, 4 and 5 of 6

Check three claims

Replace the word CLAIM in each box with a sentence from Claude's reply. Choose a different claim each time.

Check 1 
Show me exactly where in the cited source it says this: CLAIM. If the source does not say it, tell me clearly.
Check 2 
Show me exactly where in the cited source it says this: CLAIM. If the source does not say it, tell me clearly.
Check 3 
Show me exactly where in the cited source it says this: CLAIM. If the source does not say it, tell me clearly.

What to notice: Does Claude point to a real sentence, or does it admit the source does not say it?

Now open the sources yourself

No prompt here. Open the links, find the sentence, and mark each claim.

Result (Correct / Partly correct / Wrong):
Note:

Marking Guide

  • Correct The source says exactly this.
  • Partly correct The number is different, outdated or for another plan.
  • Wrong The source does not say it.
Prompt 6 of 6

Update the brief

Use what you found in your claim check.

Update the brief: remove any claim you could not verify, keep the comparison table and the source links, and mark anything uncertain with "(check)".

What to notice:

The final brief only keeps what you can stand behind.

Your cited decision brief

  • A comparison table of three options
  • One recommendation with reasons
  • A source link for every fact
  • Uncertain items marked "(check)"
  • The date you checked it
Instruction: Copy your final brief into a Google Doc or Word file and save it as Decision Brief.
You can reuse this method for any decision at work: a tool, a supplier or a course.
