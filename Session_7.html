Concept 1

Chatbot vs assistant vs agent

Chatbot

"A search box that answers one question at a time."

single-turn response
Example: "What is a pivot table?"

Assistant

"A helpful colleague who follows your standing instructions."

system prompt (custom instructions)
Example: "Always reply to customers in our company tone."

Agent

"A capable intern you give a goal to. They plan the steps, use tools, check their work and report back."

agentic workflow
Example: "Research three suppliers, compare them and draft a recommendation."
In this course you design the goal, steps and checks, and Claude carries them out while you stay in control.
Concept 2

The four parts of an agent

Goal

"What a finished job looks like."

Term: objective

Steps

"The plan: do this, then this, then this."

Term: planning

Tools

"What the intern can use: web search, files, apps."

Term: tool use

Checks

"Check the work before reporting back."

Term: feedback loop
Sessions 8-11 add assistants, web search, files and automation to these four parts.
Concept 3

Agent prompt = C-R-E-A-T-E, rearranged

C-R-E-A-T-E Framework Agent Structure
Context + Role Input (the facts and who you are)
Explicit Task Goal + Steps
Anti-Goals Checks ("do not guess")
Tone & Format Format
Examples Optional

Concept 4

Where agents fail

"Like a confident new intern who never says 'I don't know'."

Hallucination

States made-up facts with confidence.

Silent assumptions

Fills gaps without telling you.

Skipped steps

Drops a step in a long task.

Over-trust

We stop checking because it sounds right.

hallucination grounding (tying answers to the facts you gave) human-in-the-loop
You are the manager. AI drafts, you decide.

Hands-on Lab

Your scenario

You are the Operations Coordinator at Ceylon Fresh Foods (a fictional company). Every Friday you send your manager, Ms Perera, a weekly update.

Facts for this week

  • Orders received: 142 (last week: 128)
  • Late deliveries: 9 (last week: 14)
  • Customer complaints: 5 (3 about damaged packaging, 2 about late delivery)
  • New hire: Nimal started Monday in the packing team
  • Delivery van 2 is in the garage until Wednesday next week
  • Next week's priority: prepare for the Avurudu festive season orders

Rules for the lab

  • Start a new chat in Claude.
  • Send one prompt at a time and read the reply before the next.
  • Use the Copy button. Do not retype.
  • Stay in the same chat for prompts 1 to 6.
  • Plan for 6 prompts (up to 7 if you have time).
Prompt 1 of 6

The One-liner

Facts for this week: Orders received 142 (last week 128). Late deliveries 9 (last week 14). Customer complaints 5 (3 about damaged packaging, 2 about late delivery). New hire Nimal started Monday in the packing team. Delivery van 2 is in the garage until Wednesday next week. Next week's priority: prepare for the Avurudu festive season orders.

Write my weekly update email to my manager.

What to notice:

Is it clear and short? Did it add anything that was not in the facts?

Prompt 2 of 6

The Agent Prompt

Goal: write the weekly update email to my manager, Ms Perera. Steps: (1) group the facts into Results, Problems and Next week; (2) write the email; (3) check every number against the facts. Format: under 150 words, 3 short sections. Finish by listing anything you assumed. Use the same facts as before.

What to notice:

Compare with Prompt 1. What changed in the structure and the details?

Prompt 3 of 6

Check the Work

List every statement in the email that was not in my facts.

What to notice:

Did Claude find anything it invented? This is the checking step.

Prompt 4 of 6

New Task

Now use the same structure on a different task.

Goal: write a short reply to a customer whose jam order is 3 days late. Steps: (1) apologise; (2) offer 10% off the next order; (3) give a next step. Check: promise nothing else. Format: under 80 words. Customer name: Dilani.

What to notice:

Did the same structure work on a new task?

Prompt 5 of 6

Improve It

Make it warmer and add that we will confirm delivery within 24 hours.

What to notice:

A short follow-up can improve a good first draft.

Prompt 6 of 6

Save as Template

Turn my last prompt into a reusable template with blanks I can fill in next time, and add one line explaining how to use it.

What to notice:

Claude writes the template with blanks such as a name or a number of days.

Optional if you have time: Fill the blanks with real details (for example a customer name and the number of days late) and send it as one more prompt. The same template now works for any late-order reply.
Review

Check and improve

Evaluation Checklist

  • Do the numbers match my facts?
  • Did it follow the format and word limit?
  • Did it list its assumptions?
  • Is anything in it invented?
  • Is the tone right for a manager?

Pair Discussion (2 mins)

"Which prompt gave the better result, and why?"
