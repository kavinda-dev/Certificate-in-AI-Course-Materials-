Concept 1

Giving the AI a document to work from

Analogy

"Handing an intern a folder of papers instead of describing them from memory. They work from what is on the page."

Technical Reality

Context

The text the AI can see in this chat.

Grounding

Keeping the answer tied to that text.

Attachment

A file you add to the chat.

Without the document The AI guesses from general knowledge.
With the document The AI works from your actual words.
Concept 2

One document, four outputs

"An assembly line: each step uses the output of the last one."

1

Summary

"5 bullet points of what was discussed."

2

Task table

"Task, Owner, Deadline, Status."

3

Follow-up email

"Short, clear, with the table."

4

Check

"A person reads and corrects it."

workflow (a repeatable series of steps) pipeline (outputs feed the next step)
Concept 3

Tell the AI what to do when information is missing

"Like telling a new assistant: leave it blank if you are not sure."

Without a rule

"The AI fills the gap with a believable guess, such as an owner or a deadline that was never mentioned."

With a rule

"Write NOT STATED. Do not guess."

If an owner or deadline is not in the notes, write NOT STATED. Do not guess.
hallucination constraint (a rule the AI must follow)
Concept 4

What to upload, and what never to

Fine to use

"Practice documents, public information, anonymised notes, your own drafts."

Never use

"Customer personal details, passwords, private contracts, salary or HR records, confidential strategy."

Tip: Before you upload real work, remove names, numbers and details you would not want shared. This is called anonymising.
Check your workplace policy on AI tools before using real documents.
Concept 5

Connectors: giving AI access to your apps

Analogy

"Giving the intern a key to one filing cabinet, not to the whole building."

Technical Reality

Connector

A link that lets the AI read from, and sometimes act in, an app such as your calendar or files.

Permissions

What the AI is allowed to do with that access.

Four safety rules

Connect only what the task needs.
Start with read-only access.
Review before the AI sends, edits or deletes anything.
Do not connect work accounts without approval.
Available connectors depend on your account. Today the lecturer shows this, and you do not need to connect anything.

Hands-on Lab

Your scenario

You are the Operations Coordinator at Ceylon Fresh Foods (a fictional company). You attended the Tuesday operations meeting. Your manager wants a summary, a task table and a follow-up email by the end of the day.

Rules for the lab

Use at most 6 prompts
  • Plan for 6 prompts in total.
  • Stay in the same chat for all 6 prompts.
  • Use the Copy button. Do not retype.
  • Read each reply before moving on.

Prompt 1 of 6

Summary

Choose Option A or Option B. Send only one of them.

Option A: Paste the notes (main path)

Summarise these meeting notes in 5 bullet points.

MEETING NOTES - Ceylon Fresh Foods Ops Meeting
Date: Tue 14 Oct | Present: Ms Perera (manager), Kasun (logistics), Nimali (packing), Ayesh (sales), Dilhan (finance)
- ms perera opened, said festive orders will be up about 40% from next month. need to be ready
- kasun: van 2 still in garage, maybe back wed. thinks we need 1 rented van for 3 weeks. cost not known yet
- nimali: packing team short, 2 people on leave first week of Nov. wants 2 temp staff. also complaints about jar breakage, bubble wrap supplier too slow
- ayesh said big hotel client (Hotel Lagoon) wants bulk quote for 500 jars by end of next week. he'll need pricing from dilhan
- dilhan: can do pricing but needs final jar cost from supplier. will chase. budget for temp staff not approved yet, needs ms perera ok
- agreed: change packaging supplier? not decided, review again next meeting
- ms perera: wants a one page festive plan by 24 Oct. kasun + nimali to draft
- someone should update the customer FAQ about delivery times for festive season (no one volunteered)
- next meeting Tue 21 Oct 10am

Option B: Attach the file

Click the attachment (+) button, add Meeting-Notes.txt, then send this:

Summarise the attached meeting notes in 5 bullet points.

What to notice:

Does the summary mention the open decisions, such as the packaging supplier and the temp staff budget?

Prompt 2 of 6

Task Table

Now create a task table with these columns: Task | Owner | Deadline | Status. If an owner or deadline is not in the notes, write NOT STATED. Do not guess.

What to notice:

Which tasks have NOT STATED? That tells you what the meeting left unclear.

Prompt 3 of 6

Follow-up Email

Write a follow-up email to all attendees. Include the task table, the next meeting date and time, and keep it under 150 words.

What to notice:

Did the email keep the NOT STATED items, or did it quietly fill them in?

Prompt 4 of 6

Fix the Gaps

Fix the email: it must say clearly that the temp staff budget is not approved yet, and that nobody has volunteered to update the customer FAQ.

What to notice:

A short, specific correction is better than starting over.

Prompt 5 of 6

Polish

Make the email more polite and shorter, under 120 words.

What to notice:

Check that shortening did not remove an owner, a deadline or a date.

Prompt 6 of 6

Reusable Template

Turn my prompts from this chat into one reusable Meeting-to-Action template with blanks I can fill in next time, and add a one-line instruction on how to use it.

What to notice:

Claude writes a template with blanks such as meeting name, date and attendees. This becomes your kit.

Review

Check before you send

No prompt here. Read the final email and the task table against the notes.

Checklist

  • Every owner and deadline really appears in the notes
  • NOT STATED items are still marked, not filled in
  • The temp staff budget is shown as not approved
  • No invented names, numbers or dates
  • The tone suits your manager
